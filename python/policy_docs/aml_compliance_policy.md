# Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Compliance Policy

## 1. Reporting Thresholds

### 1.1 Currency Transaction Reports (CTR)
- Any single cash transaction exceeding $10,000 must be reported.
- Structured transactions (multiple transactions designed to avoid the $10,000 threshold) must also be reported.

### 1.2 Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR)
File a SAR when:
- A transaction of $5,000 or more involves suspected illegal activity.
- A transaction of $2,000 or more involves suspected money laundering.
- Any pattern of transactions that appears structured to avoid reporting thresholds.
- Transactions with no apparent business or lawful purpose.

## 2. SAR Filing Requirements

### 2.1 Timeline
- SAR must be filed within 30 calendar days of initial detection.
- If no suspect is identified, the deadline extends to 60 calendar days.
- A copy must be retained for 5 years from the filing date.

### 2.2 Required Information
- Identity of the suspect (if known).
- Description of the suspicious activity.
- Amount involved.
- Date and method of detection.
- Supporting documentation.

## 3. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) Triggers

Apply Enhanced Due Diligence when:
- Customer is from a high-risk jurisdiction.
- Transaction involves a politically exposed person (PEP).
- Customer's business is cash-intensive.
- Unusual account activity that deviates significantly from the customer profile.
- Wire transfers to or from high-risk countries.

## 4. Record Retention

- All AML-related records must be retained for a minimum of 5 years.
- CTRs: retain for 5 years from filing date.
- SARs: retain for 5 years from filing date.
- Customer identification records: retain for 5 years after account closure.

## 5. Escalation

- Level 1 (Analyst): routine monitoring and initial SAR preparation.
- Level 2 (Senior Analyst): complex investigations, cross-border transactions.
- Level 3 (AML Officer): SAR approval, regulatory correspondence, law enforcement liaison.
